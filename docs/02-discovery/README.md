# Discovery

Discovery builds the understanding required to make informed product decisions before requirements, UX or visual design begin.

The purpose of this stage is to learn about the client, product, users, market, existing systems and constraints rather than immediately deciding what should be built.

## Objectives

- Understand the client's business and product
- Understand the problem being solved
- Identify the people affected by the problem
- Review existing products, systems and content
- Understand competitors and alternatives
- Identify technical, business and operational constraints
- Identify assumptions that require validation
- Gather evidence that can inform requirements and UX decisions

## Inputs

- Completed client brief
- Existing brand assets
- Existing website or application
- Existing analytics or research
- Client and stakeholder knowledge
- Available user feedback
- Relevant competitor or market information

## Outputs

- `discovery.md`
- Research notes where required
- Identified assumptions
- Identified risks
- Initial opportunities
- Questions requiring further investigation

## Principles

Discovery should document what is known, what is assumed and what remains unknown.

Avoid designing solutions during this stage unless they are recorded explicitly as ideas or hypotheses.

Evidence should be preferred over assumptions where evidence is reasonably available.

## Exit Criteria

Move to Requirements when:

- [ ] The business and product context is understood
- [ ] The core problem is understood
- [ ] Target users have been identified
- [ ] Existing products or systems have been reviewed where applicable
- [ ] Relevant competitors or alternatives have been reviewed
- [ ] Known constraints have been documented
- [ ] Major assumptions have been identified
- [ ] Major risks have been identified
- [ ] Important unanswered questions are recorded
